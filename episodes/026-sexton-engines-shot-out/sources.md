## Disabling fire
- Rear Adm. Jeffrey Novak, deputy commander Coast Guard Pacific Area, to KFMB (CBS affiliate), article updated Dec. 16, 2025: "We can fire warning shots and then disabling fire to shoot out the engines of the fast vessel." and "We have the ability to call a non-compliant vessel and use force from an aircraft."; Cmdr. Earl Potter (Active) described three "suspected narcoterrorists" on each of two go-fasts; Munro go-fast >20,000 lb, "largest single-boat seizure in nearly 20 years" (CBS's words): https://www.cbsnews.com/news/coast-guard-cocaine-seizure-eastern-pacific-ocean-disabling-fire/ (also https://www.aol.com/articles/coast-guard-uses-disabling-fire-151247352.html)
- 14 U.S.C. 526 (summary read via WebFetch; full text not quoted): fire at a vessel liable to seizure/examination that fails to stop; warning gun normally first unless unreasonable danger; indemnity: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/14/526
- 1980 Thomas E: cutter Point Francis (82 ft), 50-ft lobster boat, Oct. 8-9, 1980; fire hose, .50 cal warning bursts, three attempts to foul the propeller; 6:02 a.m. Oct. 9 fire into hull and stern, 55 rounds; crew surrendered; >27,000 lb marijuana; "the first time since shortly after Prohibition ended, 47 years earlier": https://www.mycg.uscg.mil/News/Article/4346436/drug-smuggler-thomas-e-and-the-services-first-use-of-disabling-fire-since-prohi/

## Primary: SDNY / DEA release 26-338, Nov. 20, 2026
URL: https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/henry-richard-crespo-guzman-former-bolivian-air-force-colonel-extradited-united-states
- Crespo-Guzman, a/k/a "Colonel Crespo", 56; extradited Nov. 19, 2026 from Bolivia; arrested by Bolivian authorities Aug. 28, 2026; "will make his initial appearance" before Magistrate Judge Gary Stein Nov. 20, 2026 (release says scheduled, NOT that it happened); case assigned to District Judge Victor Marrero.
- Superseding Indictment, three counts: (1) conspiring to import cocaine into the U.S. — mandatory min 10 yrs, max life; (2) using, carrying, possessing machineguns and destructive devices during/in furtherance of the conspiracy — mandatory min 30 yrs, max life, must run consecutively to any other sentence; (3) conspiring to use and carry machineguns — max life. "The maximum potential sentences … are provided here for informational purposes only, as any sentencing of the defendant will be determined by the judge."
- Period: "From at least in or about September 2020, up to and including in or about August 2021, and while the defendant was a Colonel in the Bolivian Air Force"; "worked with others to manufacture and distribute large quantities of cocaine intended for importation to the United States"; access to airfields and military facilities incl. an Air Force Base in "Cuatro Canadas, Bolivia" (release spelling, no tilde); used it "to safeguard the transportation of cocaine loads for ultimate importation to various countries, including the United States."
- VERBATIM: "the defendant conducted meetings at the Bolivian Air Force Base while dressed in his official Bolivian Air Force Uniform, while subordinate members of the Bolivian Air Force stood guard with automatic weapons." / at one meeting he "described the Bolivian Air Force base, explained that it was part of a restricted military zone, discussed the types of aircraft it could accommodate, and detailed, in particular, the quantities and time needed to load cocaine onto aircraft" / "the defendant and his co-conspirators agreed to, and did, manufacture cocaine intended for importation to the United States." / "An image of the defendant in his official uniform captured during that meeting is depicted below" (release does NOT say who captured it, or by what means; says "During the investigation").
- Release gives NO kilograms, aircraft type, flight dates, weapons count, co-conspirator names.
- U.S. Attorney Jamie McDonald (verbatim): "Crespo-Guzman abused his position in the Bolivian military to aid drug traffickers by providing military facilities and armed personnel." "As alleged, the defendant personally participated in a cocaine importation conspiracy that was intended to bring planeloads of cocaine north for ultimate importation into the United States. The defendant is alleged to have participated in some of these meetings while in uniform and at military facilities under the armed protection of his subordinates in the Bolivian Air Force. This is the latest example of our work with the DEA to root out cocaine-fueled corruption."
- DEA Administrator Terrance C. Cole (verbatim): "This extradition sends a powerful message to those who abuse positions of public trust to facilitate the trafficking of dangerous drugs into the United States: no one is beyond the reach of American justice." "Crespo Guzmán allegedly exploited his position in the Bolivian military to protect cocaine trafficking operations that threatened American communities. DEA will relentlessly pursue those who enable these criminal organizations, regardless of their position or influence. I commend our DEA personnel and Bolivian law enforcement partners whose cooperation made this extradition possible and reflects our renewed commitment to confronting international drug trafficking."
- Thanked: U.S. "Department of War Southern Command" (script says "Southern Command"), DEA Lima Country Office, State Dept, U.S. Embassy La Paz, HSI Bolivia Office, INTERPOL Bolivia, Bolivian National Police, FELCN – GIOE (Grupo de Inteligencia y Operaciones Especiales); DOJ Office of International Affairs with Bolivia/INTERPOL Bolivia. Prosecution: National Security and International Narcotics Unit; AUSAs Hellman, Margulies, Robles, Scism.
- "The charges in the Superseding Indictment are merely accusations, and the defendant is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty."

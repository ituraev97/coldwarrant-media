## Primary — U.S. Attorney's Office, Western District of Texas
1. **"Firearms smugglers sentenced in Del Rio to combined 39.5 years in federal prison"** — USAO W.D. Tex., Thursday Nov. 12, 2026 (sentencing "on Tuesday" = Nov. 10, 2026). https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdtx/pr/firearms-smugglers-sentenced-del-rio-combined-395-years-federal-prison
   - Montelongo-Loera, 39, Dallas: 180 months; pleaded guilty Feb. 23 to conspiracy to traffic firearms; arrested July 14, 2025 after a co-conspirator was found attempting to smuggle 8 handguns and 19 magazines into Mexico.
   - Hernandez-Nino, 41, Mexican national illegally in the U.S.: 150 months; pleaded Feb. 26 to smuggling goods from the U.S. and alien in possession of a firearm.
   - Kenia Lizbeth Montelongo, 31, Montelongo-Loera's sister and Hernandez-Nino's wife: 144 months; pleaded to smuggling goods from the U.S. and sale/transfer of a firearm to a prohibited person.
   - Combined 474 months (39.5 years). Chief U.S. District Judge Alia Moses. AUSA Brett Miner. ATF with SAPD and ICE ERO. HSTF / Operation Take Back America. Case 2:25-cr-1979, USA v. Hernandez-Nino et al.
   - Aug. 2024: ATF received information that Kenia bought multiple firearms May 17–July 5, 2024, incl. seven .380 pistols and one .22 pistol; after each purchase she crossed into Mexico through Laredo within days; Montelongo-Loera, who had posted photos of himself with multiple firearms as social-media profile pictures, was recorded crossing in the same vehicle.
   - Aug. 2024 WhatsApp: a "buddy" could obtain requested firearms and he had someone to transport them; Nov. 2024 messages again discussed his gun trafficking operation.
   - July 16, 2025 ("two days later"): search of Hernandez-Nino's San Antonio residence: 24 firearms (handguns, AR-15 style rifles, a shotgun), 4,000+ rounds, multiple sets of body armor; ATF determined bound for a Mexican drug cartel. Couple arrested during the search.
   - U.S. Attorney Justin R. Simmons: "a massive case involving ten conspirators who smuggled more than 200 weapons into Mexico"; "used by cartels to terrorize Mexican citizens and war with law enforcement in an effort to maintain control of drug trafficking and human smuggling routes"; "ultimately harm American citizens trapped in the prison of addiction".
   - ATF Houston SAC Michael Weddel: "played significant roles in a coordinated operation to illegally acquire and traffic firearms intended for a Mexican drug cartel"; "seize dozens of firearms".
   - Photo: "Additional rifles, ammunition and body armor seized in San Antonio."
2. **"Mexican firearms smuggler sentenced to 15 years in federal prison, $15,000 fine in Del Rio"** — USAO W.D. Tex., March 13, 2026. https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdtx/pr/mexican-firearms-smuggler-sentenced-15-years-federal-prison-15000-fine-del-rio
   - Jose De La Cruz-Cardoza, 52, of Piedras Negras; July 12, 2025, Eagle Pass Port of Entry, outbound inspection, negative declaration for firearms, ammunition or currency over $10,000; CBP search found 8 handguns and 19 magazines.
   - Paid $150–$200 per firearm smuggled; smuggled firearms to Mexico ~10–12 previous times. Pleaded guilty in September to conspiracy to traffic firearms. 180 months; $15,000 fine appears only in headline.
   - Francisco Cardoza Jr. pleaded guilty in October (2025) to conspiracy to traffic firearms (sentencing then set for April 23).
   - Simmons: "This case is the model for how a Homeland Security Task Force should work." Weddel: "The illegal trafficking of firearms to Mexico directly fuels cartel violence."
   - Photos: handguns and magazines from Cruz-Cardoza's vehicle at Eagle Pass July 12, 2025; rifles/ammo/body armor in San Antonio; Santa Muerte statues inside Hernandez-Nino's residence (searched July 16, 2025).
3. **"Texas man found guilty in HSTF case involving large-scale cartel firearm trafficking"** — USAO W.D. Tex., May 2026 (Washington Times report dated May 5, 2026). https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdtx/pr/texas-man-found-guilty-hstf-case-involving-large-scale-cartel-firearm-trafficking
   - Bobby Brandon Galvan, 30, jury in Del Rio, two counts of firearms trafficking; Sept. 16, 2023–April 14, 2024 bought 24 AK-47 styled firearms; attempted to obliterate serial numbers; all 24 ended up in Toluca, Mexico, for use by cartels; one recovered after a shootout between cartel members and Mexican law enforcement; affiliated with La Nueva Familia Michoacana (FTO Feb. 20, 2025); one of 26 defendants.
4. **"North Carolina family members plead guilty to trafficking firearms to Mexican cartel"** — USAO W.D. Tex., Feb. 25, 2026. https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdtx/pr/north-carolina-family-members-plead-guilty-trafficking-firearms-mexican-cartel
   - Jose Manuel Najera, 54, Elon, N.C.; sons Fernando Najera-Avalos, 26, Alonso Najera-Avalos, 22; Sinaloa Cartel; Fernando bought 14 firearms over five trips June–Oct. 2024; text from unsaved Mexican number asking for 50 guns over a few months from March 2024; sons bought in N.C., father drove to Mexico usually within a week; Fernando: debt to the cartel; Alonso admitted owing $70,000; Oct. 31, 2024 outbound inspection Del Rio POE, truck flagged for suspicious crossing history; disassembled rifle and box magazine under black tarp; first denial, then said Fernando bought it for people in Mexico; all three pleaded to conspiracy to traffic firearms; up to 15 years each.

## Context
6. **GAO-21-322**, "Firearms Trafficking: U.S. Efforts to Disrupt Gun Smuggling into Mexico Would Benefit from Additional Data and Analysis", Feb. 22, 2021. https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-21-322
   - 70% of firearms reported recovered in Mexico 2014–2018 and submitted for tracing were U.S.-sourced (ATF); "Trafficking of U.S.-sourced firearms into Mexico is a national security threat"; only Mexico's federal AG office submits trace requests, ATF lacks data on thousands recovered by Mexican states; ICE analyzed 1,012 firearms seized in the U.S. but not ATF data on 56,000 U.S.-sourced firearms recovered in Mexico; ICE-led taskforces seized 1,104 firearms along the SW border FY2015–2017; CBP increased outbound inspections at some ports in FY2020.
7. **CRS IF12190**, "Gun Control: Straw Purchase and Gun Trafficking Provisions in P.L. 117-159". https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12190.html — BSCA (June 2022) created 18 U.S.C. §932 (straw purchasing) and §933 (trafficking, incl. attempts/conspiracies); up to 15 years (25 in some straw cases); before: false statements to an FFL (up to 10 years) / in required records (up to 5 years); critics: "mere paperwork violations".
8. **DOJ OPA, June 11, 2024**, "Justice Department secures more than 500 prosecutions under new firearms statutes enacted…" https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-secures-more-500-prosecutions-under-new-firearms-statutes-enacted — more than 500 defendants charged under BSCA provisions.
9. **Smith & Wesson Brands v. Estados Unidos Mexicanos** (U.S. June 5, 2025), via ABA Journal: https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/supreme-court-rejects-mexicos-suit-against-us-gun-makers-finding-problems-with-aiding-and-abetting-theory — unanimous; Kagan: Mexico "has a severe gun violence problem"; manufacturers "cannot be charged with assisting in criminal acts just because Mexican cartel members like those guns too."
10. **DVIDS 965069** (CBP, June 2, 2025) description: CBP Laredo Field Office press availability with HSI San Antonio and ATF Houston on southbound firearms interdictions, "enhanced consequences for weapons smuggling given the designation of major Mexico cartels as foreign terror organizations as well as criminal consequences for straw-purchasing firearms for others". https://www.dvidshub.net/video/965069
11. HSTF established by Executive Order 14159 (Jan. 2025) — DOJ OPA, Nov. 10, 2026 (36 indicted, S.C.): https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/multi-state-homeland-security-task-force-investigation-leads-36-indicted-drug-trafficking
